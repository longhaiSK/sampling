1 The Hansen–Hurwitz Estimator (UPSWR)

How to Do Unequal Probability Sampling?

Suppose n>1 and we sample with replacement: the selection probabilities do not change after the first draw. Let \psi_i = P(\text{select unit } i \text{ on any given draw}),\qquad \sum_{i=1}^N\psi_i=1 .

Draw n units with replacement (a unit may be drawn more than once). The sample \mathcal R (not \mathcal S) is the list of the n draws, including repeats: for example, n=10, \mathcal R=\{D,D,D,C,C,D,C,A,B,B\} A common choice, probabilities proportional to size (PPS), with a size variable x_i>0 known for every unit: \psi_i = \frac{x_i}{t_x},\qquad t_x=\sum_{k=1}^N x_k .

Drawing Probabilities in Practice

Figure 1

Divide [0,1] into segments of length \psi_1,\ldots,\psi_N. Draw u\sim\text{Uniform}(0,1) (red triangles); the segment that u falls into determines the selected unit. In R: sample(1:N, size = n, prob = psi, replace = TRUE).

Ratios b_i and the Ratio B

With \psi_i=x_i/t_x, define for every unit b_i=\frac{y_i}{x_i},\qquad B=\frac{t_y}{t_x}=\frac{\bar y_U}{\bar x_U},\qquad \bar x_U=\frac{t_x}{N}. B=\sum x_ib_i/\sum x_i is the x-weighted mean of the b_i, and \bar y_U=\bar x_U\,B,\qquad t=t_x\,B . Estimating B is enough: build \hat B, then multiply by \bar x_U or t_x.

Sampling Distribution of b_j^*

Let b_j^* be the ratio b of the unit selected on draw j (the star: it is indexed by the draw, so it is random, while b_1,\ldots,b_N are fixed numbers). It is a discrete random variable with distribution

Unit selected on draw j 1 2 \cdots N
Value of b_j^* b_1 b_2 \cdots b_N
Probability \psi_1=x_1/t_x \psi_2=x_2/t_x \cdots \psi_N=x_N/t_x

Draws are with replacement, so b_1^*,\ldots,b_n^* are IID with this distribution.

Mean and Variance of b_j^*

E(b_j^*)=\sum_{i=1}^N\psi_i\,b_i=\sum_{i=1}^N\frac{x_i}{t_x}\frac{y_i}{x_i}=\frac{t}{t_x}=B,\qquad S_{b^*}^2=V(b_j^*)=\sum_{i=1}^N\psi_i(b_i-B)^2 . A single draw is unbiased for B, and precise when the b_i are nearly equal, i.e. y_i\propto x_i.

HH Estimator of B: Mean and Variance

The HH estimator is the mean of the n IID observations b_j^* (the mean of the ratios): \boxed{\hat B_{\mathrm{HH}}=\bar b^*=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n b_j^*},\qquad E(\hat B_{\mathrm{HH}})=B, and its variance is that of one draw divided by n: V(\hat B_{\mathrm{HH}})=\frac{S_{b^*}^2}{n}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^N\psi_i(b_i-B)^2 . No finite population correction: the draws are independent.

Estimating the Variance; Mean and Total

The sample variance of the b_j^* is unbiased for S_{b^*}^2: \boxed{\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}(\hat B_{\mathrm{HH}})=\frac{s_{b^*}}{\sqrt n}},\qquad s_{b^*}^2=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^n(b_j^*-\bar b^*)^2 .

Quantity Estimate Estimated SE
Ratio B=t/t_x \hat B_{\mathrm{HH}} s_{b^*}/\sqrt n
Mean per unit \bar y_U=\bar x_UB \bar x_U\,\hat B_{\mathrm{HH}} \bar x_U\,s_{b^*}/\sqrt n
Total t=t_xB t_x\,\hat B_{\mathrm{HH}} t_x\,s_{b^*}/\sqrt n

HH Estimator in Terms of \psi_i

From \hat B_{\mathrm{HH}} (PPS, \psi_i=x_i/t_x, so t_x/x_i=1/\psi_i): \begin{aligned} \hat t_{\mathrm{HH}}&=t_x\hat B_{\mathrm{HH}}\\ &=t_x\,\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n b_j^*\\ &=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n t_x\frac{y_j^*}{x_j^*}\\ &=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{y_j^*}{\psi_j^*} \end{aligned}

The Weight of the HH Estimator

Writing the sum over the draws in \mathcal R (repeats included): \hat t_{\mathrm{HH}}=\frac1n\sum_{i\in\mathcal R}\frac{y_i}{\psi_i}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal R}w_i\,y_i, \boxed{w_i=\frac{1}{n\,\psi_i}}

  • Each term y_i/\psi_i is an estimate of the total t; the information about t_x is carried in \psi_i.
  • This holds for any selection probabilities \psi_i.
  • Unit i is expected to appear n\psi_i times, so each draw of unit i represents 1/(n\psi_i) units.
  • With PPS, w_i=t_x/(n\,x_i): large units get small weights.

Variance of the HH Estimator in Terms of \psi_i

From V(\hat B_{\mathrm{HH}})=S_{b^*}^2/n, using t_xb_i=y_i/\psi_i and t_xB=t, so that t_x(b_i-B)=y_i/\psi_i-t: \begin{aligned} V(\hat t_{\mathrm{HH}})&=t_x^2\,V(\hat B_{\mathrm{HH}})=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^N\psi_i\,t_x^2(b_i-B)^2\\ &=\boxed{\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^N\psi_i\Big(\frac{y_i}{\psi_i}-t\Big)^2} \end{aligned} Likewise t_x(b_j^*-\bar b^*)=y_j^*/\psi_j^*-\hat t_{\mathrm{HH}}, so the estimated variance t_x^2s_{b^*}^2/n is \hat V(\hat t_{\mathrm{HH}})=\boxed{\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{j=1}^n\Big(\frac{y_j^*}{\psi_j^*}-\hat t_{\mathrm{HH}}\Big)^2} Both hold for any \psi_i, with no finite population correction.

Equivalence: \psi_i as a Normalized Size

Take the normalized size \tilde x_i=\psi_i, so that t_{\tilde x}=\sum_i\tilde x_i=1 and \tilde x_i/t_{\tilde x}=\psi_i. Its ratios and population ratio are \tilde b_i=\frac{y_i}{\tilde x_i}=\frac{y_i}{\psi_i},\qquad \tilde B=\frac{t}{t_{\tilde x}}=t , so the HH estimator of \tilde B is the HH estimator of the total: \hat{\tilde B}_{\mathrm{HH}}=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{y_j^*}{\psi_j^*}=\hat t_{\mathrm{HH}},\qquad \hat t_{\mathrm{HH}}=t_{\tilde x}\,\hat{\tilde B}_{\mathrm{HH}}=\hat{\tilde B}_{\mathrm{HH}} .

  • With a normalized size, the “ratio” is the total.
  • Conversely, \psi_i=x_i/t_x gives y_j^*/\psi_j^*=t_x\,b_j^*, so \hat t_{\mathrm{HH}}=t_x\,\bar b^*.
  • Rescaling a size variable to total 1 does not change the estimator of the total.

4 Comparison of the HH, Ratio and Average Estimators of the Total

Three Estimators of the Total

Residuals about the line through the origin: d_i=y_i-Bx_i,\qquad B=\frac{t}{t_x},\qquad \sum_{i=1}^N d_i=0 .

  • Sample average (SRS, ignores x_i): \hat t_{\mathrm{avg}}=N\bar y, \displaystyle V(\hat t_{\mathrm{avg}})=\Big(1-\frac nN\Big)\frac{N^2S_y^2}{n}.
  • Ratio (SRS, x_i at the estimation stage): \hat t_r=t_x\,\bar y/\bar x; an SRS selects every unit with probability 1/N, so V(\hat t_r)\approx\Big(1-\frac nN\Big)\frac{N^2S_d^2}{n}\approx\Big(1-\frac nN\Big)\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^N\frac{d_i^2}{1/N}.
  • HH (UPSWR, x_i at the design stage, \psi_i=x_i/t_x): each draw deviates by a scaled residual, \frac{y_i}{\psi_i}-t=\frac{d_i}{\psi_i} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad V(\hat t_{\mathrm{HH}})=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^N\frac{d_i^2}{\psi_i}.

Comparison of the Three Estimators

Estimator Estimator of t Variance x_i used at Unbiased?
Sample average (SRS) N\bar y \Big(1-\dfrac nN\Big)\dfrac{N^2S_y^2}{n} not used yes
Ratio (SRS) t_x\,\dfrac{\bar y}{\bar x} \approx\Big(1-\dfrac nN\Big)\dfrac1n\sum\limits_{i=1}^N\dfrac{d_i^2}{1/N} estimation stage slightly biased
HH (UPSWR) \dfrac1n\sum\limits_{j=1}^n\dfrac{y_j^*}{\psi_j^*} \dfrac1n\sum\limits_{i=1}^N\dfrac{d_i^2}{\psi_i} design stage exactly unbiased

For the mean, divide the estimators by N and the variances by N^2.

On a Common Scale

Ignoring the fpc, each variance is \frac1n\sum_i (squared deviation)/(selection probability of unit i):

Estimator Term of unit i Same, as N\times (squared deviation) / (relative size) Spread of y about
Sample average \dfrac{(y_i-\bar y_U)^2}{1/N} \dfrac{N(y_i-\bar y_U)^2}{1} the flat line \bar y_U
Ratio \dfrac{d_i^2}{1/N} \dfrac{N\,d_i^2}{1} the line y=Bx
HH \dfrac{d_i^2}{\psi_i} \dfrac{N\,d_i^2}{x_i/\bar x_U}=\dfrac{N\,d_i^2}{w^x_i} the line y=Bx

Since \psi_i=x_i/t_x=\dfrac{1}{N}\dfrac{x_i}{\bar x_U}: the three terms differ only in the divisor, 1 for SRS, w^x_i=x_i/\bar x_U for HH.

  • Ratio vs average: y_i-\bar y_U=d_i+B(x_i-\bar x_U). If y\approx Bx, the ratio estimator removes the spread due to x; if y is unrelated to x, d_i adds it and the average wins.
  • HH vs ratio: the same d_i^2, divided by \psi_i instead of 1/N. Units with \psi_i>1/N (larger than average) are downweighted, since they are already drawn more often.

When Does UPS Beat the Ratio Estimator?

V(\hat t_r)-V(\hat t_{\mathrm{HH}})\approx\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^N d_i^2\Big(N-\frac1{\psi_i}\Big) =\frac Nn\sum_{i=1}^N d_i^2\Big(1-\frac{\bar x_U}{x_i}\Big), so HH wins exactly when the units with large residuals are larger than average: \boxed{H_d=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^N d_i^2}{\sum_{i=1}^N d_i^2/x_i}>\bar x_U} If d_i^2\approx\sigma^2x_i^{\gamma}:

Spread of y about the line y=Bx \gamma Better
constant 0 ratio (SRS)
\mathrm{SD}\propto\sqrt{x}, i.e. V(y_i)\propto x_i 1 tie
\mathrm{SD}\propto x: constant coefficient of variation 2 HH (UPSWR)

Revenues, acreages and counts often have a roughly constant CV (\gamma\approx2): PPS works well for them.

Three Population Shapes

Plot y against x (census or pilot): if the scatter fans out in proportion to x, sample with PPS; if its width stays about the same, use an SRS with the ratio estimator.

5 One-Stage Cluster Sampling with PPS

Change of Notation

Clusters (psus) are the units: y_i\to t_i (cluster total), x_i\to M_i (cluster size), t_x\to M_0=\sum_{i=1}^NM_i,\qquad \bar x_U\to\bar M_U=\frac{M_0}{N},\qquad \psi_i=\frac{M_i}{M_0}. The ratio of cluster i is its mean per element (ssu-level mean) b_i=\frac{t_i}{M_i}=\bar y_i ,\qquad B=\frac{t}{M_0}=\bar y_U\ \ (\text{mean per element}).

Estimates for One-Stage Cluster Sampling

Then \hat{\bar y}_{\mathrm{HH}}=\hat B_{\mathrm{HH}}=\dfrac1n\sum_{j=1}^n\bar y_j^*: the plain average of the sampled cluster means (large clusters are already selected more often), with \widehat{\mathrm{SE}}=s_{\bar y^*}/\sqrt n, s_{\bar y^*}^2=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_j(\bar y_j^*-\hat{\bar y}_{\mathrm{HH}})^2.

Quantity Estimate Estimated SE
Mean per element \bar y_U=t/M_0 \hat{\bar y}_{\mathrm{HH}} s_{\bar y^*}/\sqrt n
Mean per cluster \bar t_U=t/N \hat{\bar t}_{\mathrm{HH}}=\bar M_U\,\hat{\bar y}_{\mathrm{HH}} \bar M_U\,s_{\bar y^*}/\sqrt n
Total t \hat t_{\mathrm{HH}}=M_0\,\hat{\bar y}_{\mathrm{HH}} M_0\,s_{\bar y^*}/\sqrt n

In R, cluster_upswr(ti, Mi, N, M0, estimate) calls upswr_est with y=t, x=M, and reports in this notation; estimate is "ybar", "tbar" or "total".

6 Two-Stage Cluster Sampling with Replacement

Setup and Estimators

Select n clusters with replacement, \psi_i=M_i/M_0, then take an SRS of m_i elements from the cluster on every draw: a cluster drawn twice gets two independent subsamples. The cluster mean b_i=t_i/M_i is not observed, but estimated by the subsample mean \bar y_i, and \hat t_i=M_i\bar y_i.

The one-stage formulas carry over with \bar y_j^* now the subsample mean of draw j: \hat{\bar y}_{\mathrm{HH}}=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n\bar y_j^*,\qquad \widehat{\mathrm{SE}}(\hat{\bar y}_{\mathrm{HH}})=\frac{s_{\bar y^*}}{\sqrt n},\qquad s_{\bar y^*}^2=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^n(\bar y_j^*-\hat{\bar y}_{\mathrm{HH}})^2 , mean per cluster \hat{\bar t}_{\mathrm{HH}}=\bar M_U\hat{\bar y}_{\mathrm{HH}} and total \hat t_{\mathrm{HH}}=M_0\hat{\bar y}_{\mathrm{HH}} with SEs multiplied by the same constants (each draw estimates the total by \hat t_j^*/\psi_j^*=M_0\bar y_j^*).

Note

No second-stage variance formula is needed: the draws are independent, and s_{\bar y^*}^2 captures both the choice of clusters and the choice of elements within them.

7 Unequal Probability Sampling Without Replacement (UPSWO)

The Horvitz-Thompson Estimator

Select n distinct units with inclusion probabilities \pi_i=P(i\in\mathcal S), \sum_{i=1}^N\pi_i=n. For \pips, \pi_i=n\,x_i/t_x=n\psi_i. The Horvitz-Thompson (HT) estimator weights each unit by 1/\pi_i: \boxed{\hat t_{\mathrm{HT}}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}\frac{y_i}{\pi_i}} Unbiased: with I_i=1 if i\in\mathcal S (and E(I_i)=\pi_i), \hat t_{\mathrm{HT}}=\sum_{i=1}^N I_i\,y_i/\pi_i and E(\hat t_{\mathrm{HT}})=\sum_{i=1}^N\frac{y_i}{\pi_i}E(I_i)=\sum_{i=1}^N y_i=t .

Mean and Ratio from the HT Total

Mean per unit: \hat t_{\mathrm{HT}}/N. With \pi_i\propto x_i, the ratio is the HT total over t_x: \hat B_{\mathrm{HT}}=\frac{\hat t_{\mathrm{HT}}}{t_x}=\frac1n\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}b_i , \qquad \widehat{\mathrm{SE}}(\hat B_{\mathrm{HT}})=\frac{\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}(\hat t_{\mathrm{HT}})}{t_x}.

Variances of the HT Estimator

With joint inclusion probabilities \pi_{ij} (fixed sample size), Sen-Yates-Grundy: \hat V_{\mathrm{SYG}}=\frac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}\frac{\pi_i\pi_j-\pi_{ij}}{\pi_{ij}}\Big(\frac{y_i}{\pi_i}-\frac{y_j}{\pi_j}\Big)^2 . Approximations that need only \pi_i, with u_i=y_i/\pi_i:

  • Hájek: \displaystyle\hat V_{\mathrm H}=\frac{n}{n-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}(1-\pi_i)(u_i-A)^2,\quad A=\frac{\sum_{\mathcal S}(1-\pi_i)u_i}{\sum_{\mathcal S}(1-\pi_i)}
  • With-replacement (WR): treat the sample as n draws with \psi_i=\pi_i/n, \displaystyle\hat V_{\mathrm{WR}}=\frac{n}{n-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}\Big(u_i-\frac{\hat t_{\mathrm{HT}}}{n}\Big)^2, which ignores the factors 1-\pi_i and is a little too large.

For the mean divide the SE by N; for \hat B_{\mathrm{HT}} divide by t_x.

Example: HT Estimate and Variances

8 When to Use UPS

UPS Is Risky Unless y Is Tied to the Size Variable

The gain depends on the residuals d_i=y_i-Bx_i being small, i.e. y_i roughly proportional to x_i; UPS beats the SRS ratio estimator only when the spread about the line grows with the size (H_d>\bar x_U). Otherwise,

  • the estimate can be much worse than a plain SRS average;
  • a unit with small x_i but large y_i gets a huge weight 1/\psi_i (1/\pi_i), so the estimates are unstable and the estimated SE tends to be too small (intervals under-cover).

UPS is used mostly when the size is a good proxy of what is measured: selecting the clusters of a multi-stage survey proportional to households or students, auditing by monetary value, surveys of establishments by employment. If the size is only loosely related to y, use SRS or stratified sampling, with a ratio or regression estimator if x_i is known.
